### 1. Why Algebraic Effects?

 Score: 134 Comments: 59 [Link](https://antelang.org/blog/why_effects/)

 > Algebraic effects are a powerful programming feature that enables user-definable control flow, cleaner APIs, and dependency injection by treating effects as resumable exceptions. They allow implementing various language features (like generators, exceptions, and async) as libraries while avoiding the "function color" problem. Effects also ensure purity, enable replayability, and support capability-based security by tracking side effects in type signatures.

### 28. The metre originated in the French Revolution

 Score: 97 Comments: 158 [Link](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2025-05-20/metre-treaty-anniversary-metric-system-measurement-metrology/105302024)

 > The metre originated during the French Revolution as a universal measurement tied to nature, initially defined as one 10-millionth of the distance from the North Pole to the equator. The 1875 Metre Convention standardized it globally, but its definition evolved—first using platinum bars, then krypton light, and finally the speed of light in 1983. While the metric system is widely adopted, some countries like the US still use imperial units in daily life.
