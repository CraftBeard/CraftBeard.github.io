### 24. Why cryptography is not based on NP-complete problems

 Score: 81 Comments: 37 [Link](https://blintzbase.com/posts/cryptography-is-not-based-on-np-hard-problems/)

 > Cryptography relies on average-case hardness, requiring randomly generated problem instances (like factoring large primes in RSA) to be concretely hard (e.g., >2^128 operations). NP-complete problems, while hard in the worst case, may have many easy random instances, making them unsuitable. Cryptographic security demands that most instances are hard, not just some. Though lattice-based schemes link average and worst-case hardness, NP-complete problems lack such guarantees. Thus, cryptography uses specialized problems rather than NP-complete ones.
