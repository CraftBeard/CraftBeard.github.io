 > During World War II, telegrams marked with "This telegram must be closely paraphrased before being communicated to anyone" were encrypted messages. This instruction was a security measure to prevent enemy cryptanalysis. If the exact same plaintext was transmitted multiple times, it could help an adversary break the code. ### 8. C++: Strongly Happens Before?

 Score: 7 Comments: discuss [Link](https://nekrozqliphort.github.io/posts/happens-b4/)

 > The article explains that "strongly happens before" was introduced in C++20 to fix a memory model issue where a valid execution on architectures like Power created a cycle in the happens-before graph for `memory_order_seq_cst` operations. This cycle prevented establishing a single total order, which is required by the standard. Instead of forcing a performance penalty on all implementations, the C++ committee chose to weaken the rule, allowing such executions by only requiring the stronger "strongly happens before" relation (not the regular "happens before") to constrain the total order.
